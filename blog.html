Playstation 5 Review

The PS5 is still the best all-round console you can buy in 2026 — a machine that's aged gracefully into its prime.

The DualSense controller alone is worth the price of admission. Haptic feedback and adaptive triggers genuinely change how games feel — you sense tension in a bowstring, rumble across different surfaces, and feel each impact. It's the feature that still makes PlayStation feel a generation ahead of the competition.

The Good

  • Load times are essentially gone — the custom SSD turns "waiting" into a distant memory, and fast travel is near-instant.
  • A deep, exclusive-heavy library - God of War, Horizon, Demon's Souls and more, plus most PS4 games run on it, often with improved performance.⁠
  • Backward compatibility done right — your old disc and digital library carries over, which is a huge value win.
  • The Slim model is smaller, quieter, and runs cooler than the launch console, making it an easier fit in any setup.⁠

The Bad

  • Still pricey, especially once you factor in a second controller and a few games.
  • Storage fills up fast — 1TB sounds like a lot until modern games each eat 100GB+.
  • The library is mature, so if you've owned a PS5 for years, there's less new-exclusive reason to upgrade than there was at launch.

Bottom line: if you don't own one yet, it's a fantastic time to jump in — the ecosystem, exclusives, and controller make it the most complete package in gaming right now. If you already have one, there's little reason to rush any upgrade.⁠

Samsung Galaxy A36 5G Review

The Galaxy A36 is the budget-king of 2026 — a phone that gives you flagship-feeling daily use without the flagship price.

Samsung nailed the essentials here. The 6.7" Super AMOLED display is bright (up to 1900 nits peak), smooth at 120Hz, and protected by Gorilla Glass Victus+ — it looks and feels far more expensive than it is

The Good

  • Excellent battery life — the 5,000 mAh cell easily gets through a full day, and 45W charging tops it up fast (66% in about 30 minutes).⁠
  • A capable 50MP main camera with OIS — solid daylight shots, good stabilisation, and 4K video on both the main and selfie cameras.
  • Long software support — up to six major OS updates means this phone stays current for years, a big deal at this price.⁠
  • IP67 water resistance — rare in this segment and genuinely useful.

The Bad

  • No microSD slot — storage is fixed, so pick the 256GB version if you store a lot.
  • The Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 handles everyday tasks and light gaming smoothly, but it's not a gaming powerhouse — heavy titles will make it sweat.⁠
  • The macro camera is basically a gimmick; the ultrawide is decent but nothing special.

Bottom line: for everyday use — social media, streaming, photos, and all-day battery — the A36 punches well above its price. It's the phone to beat in the budget segment, as long as you don't need cutting-edge gaming performance.⁠
